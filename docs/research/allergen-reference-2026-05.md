Global Allergen Identification and Labeling Reference Guide

1. Introduction to Regulatory Allergen Architecture

Strategic ingredient identification is the cornerstone of regulatory compliance and proactive risk mitigation. In the global food supply chain, the ability to distinguish between definite allergen sources, ambiguous labeling terms, and high-risk "trap" products defines a manufacturer’s legal standing and a product's safety profile. Regulatory obligations are dictated by jurisdictional mandates: the United States operates under the Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act (FALCPA) and the FASTER Act, with a complex jurisdictional split between the FDA and USDA. In contrast, the European Union adheres to the broader Annex II requirements of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011. Navigating these frameworks requires a precise understanding of how an ingredient’s "common or usual name" may legally conceal allergen sources, particularly under USDA rules for non-meat components.

The following analysis provides a specialized reference for these categories, beginning with the complex regulatory landscape of Gluten-containing cereals.

2. Gluten (Wheat, Barley, Rye, and Derivatives)

Gluten-containing cereals demand the most rigorous cross-referencing due to their industrial ubiquity and the disparate labeling mandates of the FDA and USDA. While the FDA requires the disclosure of wheat in all instances, USDA-regulated products (meat, poultry, and egg products) require only the "common or usual name." This creates a regulatory gap where wheat-derived carriers for flavorings or starches may not be identified as wheat unless the manufacturer voluntarily adopts FALCPA standards.

3. Milk and Dairy Protein

11. Regulatory Framework Comparison: US vs. EU

The divergence between the US "Major 9" and the EU's Annex II requirements creates significant compliance complexity. While the US focuses on clear language for nine categories, the EU requires the highlighting of 14 allergens and allows for specific scientific exemptions for processed derivatives.
