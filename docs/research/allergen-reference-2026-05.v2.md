1. Gluten and Wheat-Derived Prolamins

Gluten labeling is uniquely complex due to the distinct immune responses triggered by specific prolamin proteins and the high prevalence of agricultural cross-contact in grain processing. Regulatory standards vary significantly regarding the use of mechanical sorting versus strict purity protocols for oats.

10. Comparative Regulatory Analysis: US vs. EU

International compliance requires understanding the stricter EU standards, particularly regarding gluten-containing grains and formatting. These differences are critical for the safety of travelers and the accuracy of imported product labels.

TABLE 4 — US vs. EU Disclosure Rule Differences

Allergen / Category	Required under US FALCPA?	Required under EU 1169/2011?	Key Formatting / Classification Difference
Wheat	Yes	Yes	EU requires emphasized text (bold/italics) in ingredient list
Oats	No (Not an allergen)	Yes (Gluten cereal)	EU Annex II classifies oats as a cereal containing gluten
Milk	Yes	Yes	EU mandates bolding; US allows "Contains" statement
Peanut	Yes	Yes	EU requires clear emphasis in the ingredient list
Tree Nuts	Yes	Yes	Both require species disclosure; EU mandates bold/italics
Egg	Yes	Yes	Standardized disclosure; EU requires emphasized text
Soy	Yes	Yes	Refined oil exempt in US; EU requires clear bolding
Sesame	Yes	Yes	Added as 9th US allergen under FASTER Act in 2023
Fish	Yes	Yes	Disclosure required; EU mandates bold/italics
Shellfish	Yes (Crustacean)	Yes (Crustacean/Mollusc)	EU Annex II includes molluscs; US only crustacean
