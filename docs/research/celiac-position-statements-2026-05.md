**Oat Regulation in the United States**
Under the FDA's Food Allergen Labeling and Consumer Protection Act (FALCPA), **oats are not recognized as a major food allergen** and are not subject to mandatory allergen disclosure. While oats are natively gluten-free, they are highly prone to agricultural cross-contact because they are routinely grown, harvested, and milled using the same equipment as wheat, barley, and rye. 

For oats to be legally labeled "gluten-free" in the US, they must meet the FDA standard of containing less than 20 parts per million (ppm) of gluten. Manufacturers achieve this in one of two ways:
*   **Purity Protocol:** A strict set of standards designed to prevent the oats from coming into contact with gluten at any stage of the agricultural and manufacturing process.
*   **Mechanical/Optical Sorting:** Using machines and human inspectors to sort out errant gluten-containing grains from standard oats. 

Oats do not need to be certified by a third party to carry a gluten-free label as long as they meet the <20 ppm threshold.

**Oat Regulation in the European Union**
The EU takes a much stricter regulatory approach to oats. Under EU Regulation 1169/2011 (Annex II), **oats are explicitly classified as a "cereal containing gluten" and a priority food allergen**. Because of this classification, whenever oats are present in a prepackaged food product in the EU, they must be clearly emphasized (such as with bolding, italics, or highlighting) in the ingredients list.
